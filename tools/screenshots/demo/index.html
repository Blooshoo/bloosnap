Local capybara declared mayor after landslide victory

In a stunning upset, a capybara named Gerald has won the mayoral race in a town of 4,200 people. Officials say the vote was "not even close," citing Gerald's calm demeanor and total absence of scandal.

"He just sits there and everyone feels better," said one resident. Campaign promises include more naps, shared hot springs, and a strict no-fuss policy for the town council.

Gerald's first act in office was to rename the town square "Warm Rock Plaza" and install a small pond. A spokesperson confirmed the pond is "mostly for Gerald, but everyone can look at it."

Reactions

Neighboring towns have reportedly begun lobbying for their own capybaras. Experts warn the supply is limited and note that, historically, nobody has ever regretted a capybara.

Meanwhile, the opposition candidate conceded graciously and asked only that the pond have a second, smaller rock. Gerald did not respond, which his team described as "consistent with his brand."

What's next

The mayor's office says Gerald will hold weekly "Q&A naps" starting next month. Residents are encouraged to submit questions in advance, quietly.

Analysts expect the capybara to remain popular through at least the next three seasons, assuming the pond stays warm and the grass stays long.
